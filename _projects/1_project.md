## Background

Enterprise adoption of generative AI creates new channels for accidental and deliberate data exposure. Prompts, uploaded files, copied snippets, source code, browser sessions, IDE activity, RAG pipelines, and agentic tools can all become leakage paths for credentials, regulated data, and internal intellectual property.

## Problem Statement

The core challenge is to enforce privacy and security controls close to the user without routing all content through a central monitoring system. Effective protection needs to be fast enough for real-time workflows, accurate enough to reduce alert fatigue, and auditable enough for enterprise governance.

## Focus Areas

- **On-device sensitive-data detection** for personally identifiable information, credentials, secrets, and high-risk text patterns
- **Local-first enforcement** through redaction, blocking, warning, and audit controls
- **Threat modelling** for prompt leakage, source-code exposure, insider risk, and shadow AI adoption
- **Secure workflow coverage** across browsers, copilots, IDEs, document flows, RAG pipelines, and agentic tooling

## My Role

As **Research Scientist - AI Data Security and Privacy** at GuardlyData, I:

- Build and fine-tune NER and secret-detection models for on-device inference
- Design product-facing security architecture for detection, policy enforcement, and auditing
- Translate research findings into implementation requirements, roadmaps, and technical documentation
- Support evaluation pipelines for sensitive-data detection quality and operational usability

## Current Direction

This work sits at the intersection of AI security, privacy engineering, and practical enterprise controls. The emphasis is on deployable systems that reduce risky data movement without breaking the speed and utility that make AI tools attractive in the first place.
